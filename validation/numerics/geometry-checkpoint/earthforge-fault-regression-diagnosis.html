The completed experiment uses physical-coordinate fault cuts and placement costs based on available gaps. It improves global 40–20 m travel agreement, but worsens 20–10 m travel agreement and thickness agreement. It also fails a new analytical fault-side test. These are reasons to withhold the change even though its sediment budgets close.

What the diagnosis establishes

  1. Fault-side identity cannot be recovered from a parcel centroid. An independent flat–ramp–flat example keeps all true material on the hanging-wall side while its exact centroid falls on the other side. Reclassifying a translated rectangle around that centroid incorrectly stops material on a later slip.

    This proves a limitation of the replacement geometry. It does not by itself prove that every stored-coordinate versus offset-coordinate discrepancy in a coupled run is a wrong-side transport error.

  2. Storage placement affects later accommodation and deposition. In the A diagnostic, a 36.016 m storage-surface difference develops near the right boundary before the troublesome bed is deposited; the first transported rock markers differ by about 0.000091 m. The B diagnostic likewise links a storage-floor jump to a later deposit-thickness discrepancy.

    The first rock marker is not an authoritative surface at the storage column x when it has a horizontal offset. A surface-only replacement is therefore insufficient.

  3. The worst fine-grid C travel discrepancy is generated by coupled operations. At x=3600 m, the current 20–10 m difference ends at −5.585599 ms. Step 19 thrust slip contributes −4.135191 ms to the difference, followed by additional compaction amplification.

    The recorded material flow offsets agree with an independent affine flow calculation. Closing the sediment budget and computing those offsets correctly does not ensure a consistent material geometry through later processes.

  4. Compaction needs a common material-and-fault mapping. A two-bed analytical fixture begins with coherent, nonoverlapping physical beds but different storage offsets. Current compaction moves a point from 1 m above a fault to 4.5 m below it while the fault stays fixed.

    Persistent fault-side labels alone would not reconcile this mapping inconsistency or recover a lost material footprint.

Conservation and tests

All 4,494 budget events close across 36 runs. The independent audit checks total sediment, bed inventories and material components: 755,160 scalar balances, zero failures and zero repair material added. The maximum absolute scalar residual is 1.2647361e-06 m² under the unchanged tolerance of 10⁻⁶ m² plus 10⁻¹⁰ times the balance scale. Conservation is necessary; it does not prove correct placement.

  • Main repository: the new tests/thrust-side-continuity.test.mjs passes 3/3 cases. It checks same-fault motion, independent final centroids, grains, composition, burial/loading history, unchanged input and two-step budgets. All 68 pre-existing named files were verified unchanged.
  • Rejected candidate: the same new guard fails 3/3 cases. A broad batch of 172 tests passes. A separate targeted basin batch passes 12/13; its remaining source-parcel mapping assertion fails. Six additional heavy basin tests were not run after rejection. These overlapping batches do not constitute a full-suite pass.
  • Analytical counterexamples: independent tests establish the nonconvex side-classification and differential-offset compaction problems. Passing tests that demonstrate a limitation is not evidence that the simulator has resolved it.

What a valid repair must do next

The next implementation needs an authoritative representation of where sediment actually lies and which fault side it occupies after deformation. Transport, compaction, deposition, erosion and fault geometry must use compatible physical mappings. A centroid-only classifier, a first-marker surface adjustment, or a side-label-only patch does not satisfy that requirement.

Acceptance criteria for that representation change
  1. Pass this strengthened mobility/final-motion guard and the separate independent intermediate-bend geometry proof, with conservation and source purity. Correct means alone must not authorize reclassification of lost support.
  2. Define the authoritative physical-support/topology contract. Preserve side information under same-fault flow through a coherent footprint or equivalent metadata; never infer true side solely from a centroid replacement rectangle.
  3. Resolve or explicitly bound the proven differential-offset compaction counterexample. A material point and its fault must follow a compatible common physical map if side invariance is claimed; immutable labels alone do not repair this.
  4. Specify and test membership/support propagation through exact slicing, raster/rebuild, coalescing, compaction, subsidence, collapse, new deposition, erosion, incoming continuation, and later faults. Opposite memberships must not merge; basement metadata cannot silently disappear.
  5. Retain every positive distinct material component, grains/composition and irreversible history; maintain exact support/capacity consistency and explicit boundary ledgers. No silent trimming or history averaging to obtain feasibility.
  6. Demonstrate bounded part count, successful full-depth runs, and memory/runtime within the established environment; avoid recurring footprint or fragment explosion.
  7. Run the unchanged full test suite and the frozen 12-case, 36-run 40/20/10 m synthetic comparison, with all budgets, complete paired coverage, outliers and all metrics disclosed. Keep holdout and observational arrays untouched.
  8. Tie release claims to demonstrated results. Mixed grid agreement can support a narrowly described correctness repair, but cannot establish that the requested travel-time/thickness regressions are fixed; unresolved regressions and unvalidated design work must remain explicit.
