Sediment and fault geometry: progress, with the production repair still open

The new geometry path is not ready to replace the simulator. 76 focused checks pass, but the coupled integration probe still rejects an unresolved shared interface. The original simulator remains unchanged. The holdout was untouched.

What was implemented and tested

The isolated candidate retains convex material regions, transfers grain amounts by geometric overlap, and maps entire fault segments through the same piecewise affine compaction regions. It separates physical vertical profiles from area-based sediment inventory. Boundary continuation, subsidence, erosion and partial-width deposition have analytical fixtures.

Tests cover shared bed contacts, a nonconvex hanging wall, composition and burial memory, tiny fault segments, positive thin support, open-boundary grain accounts, source immutability and explicit rejection of geometry the representation cannot resolve. Strict TypeScript checking also passes. These checks validate individual components; they do not validate a complete coupled simulation.

Several concrete prototype defects were fixed: roundoff slivers from ghost placement, inconsistent shared-edge interpolation, and deletion of short positive fault intervals. Unsupported sub-ULP mesh interfaces are rejected rather than silently discarded.

Why integration remains gated

The first coupled A trial at 40 m transports 25,343 source regions. Its total area is 60,374,400 m² and open-boundary grain residual is 7.07e-11 m². Global conservation passes, while shared-interface consistency remains unresolved.

Latest admission result: Owned physical partition has a gap or overlap (-2.842170943040401e-14m)

Sampled interval-union gaps and overlaps are approximately 7.2e-12 m and 4.97e-12 m. They are floating-point scale, but indiscriminate snapping would also erase legitimate tiny geometry. The remaining representation needs canonical shared nodes and edge intersections throughout transport, not independently reconstructed coordinates.

Nonuniform compaction also adds bends to an active fault. Its subsequent flow must be validated against the complete mapped curve. The current few-domain fault-bend flow cannot simply be assumed valid after arbitrary subdivision. The shared-face compaction prototype uses an explicit lateral closure; its constitutive residual still requires an acceptance study.

Research basis and limits

The implementation follows established conservative geometric remapping and multi-material interface reconstruction principles. Material centroids are insufficient to determine shape; material regions and interfaces must be retained or reconstructed with an explicit numerical contract. The papers motivate the approach, but do not validate this unfinished EarthForge implementation.

  • Conservative rezoning (remapping) for general quadrilateral meshes
  • Reconstruction of multi-material interfaces from moment data
  • Portage concepts: conservative remapping
